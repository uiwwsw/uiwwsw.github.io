# Article discovery, resumed sessions and idle guidance — 2026-09-10

## Findings

The old UI had three different visibility rules. Point shaders faded by camera-space depth and screen edge; picking used Euclidean distance and excluded Earth's entire projected disc, even for foreground stars; title cards always extended to the right and were dropped against coarse percentage-based UI boundaries. Titles were also withheld behind topic-centroid cards until approximately 41% travel. A visible point was therefore not a guarantee of a title, and the topic centroid was not a real article position.

The installed Fiber implementation resets `clock.elapsedTime` to zero in `setFrameloop` when switching between `never` and `always`. The site makes that switch on document hide/resume, but ArticleSky retained its previous absolute `lastCheck` and label retirement timestamps. After a long session, its `elapsedTime - lastCheck < 0.2` gate could remain closed for as long as the earlier session; stale labels then disappeared as the camera moved without being replaced. A refresh remounted the timestamps and temporarily masked the issue.

## Changes

- Every title is now attached to a real article, available just after 12% route progress. Topic nebulae stay decorative; filtering and the full archive still cover every article.
- Bodies update first, then the camera and its world matrix, then projection/picking. Hit coordinates refresh every frame for the active sector (at most 96 stars; candidate caps stay 36/64). Shader and CPU share near/edge fade constants. A ray/sphere test distinguishes stars in front of Earth from stars occluded behind it.
- Title layout remains bounded to 3 mobile / 5 desktop cards, checked five times per second. Cards try either side and upper/lower corners of the same star, avoiding measured header, heading, rail, deck, guide and credit rectangles plus Earth. A small connector identifies the real anchor; no card is detached into a screen-fixed fake star. Existing labels retain priority, hovered stars can take priority, and retiring titles fade for 0.45 seconds before replacement. Updated placement snapshots are retained instead of stale copies.
- UI scheduling accumulates finite bounded frame deltas, independent of the resettable Fiber clock and of paused ambient motion. Hidden rendering naturally stops updates without resetting accumulated UI time; resuming cannot wait for an old absolute timestamp. Ambient diagnostic reporting uses a local clock too.
- The native range input is 52px high with a 44×44px thumb hit area, a subtle 22px halo and filled track. It retains normal keyboard handling, a visible focus outline and accessible value text, with 0.1% steps. Its parent is explicitly isolated from sky gestures. The larger target does not reintroduce one-finger zoom or change pinch/wheel gains or discovery pressure.
- After the world settles, initial inactivity of 4 seconds shows guidance. Any deliberate pointer-down, key, wheel or flight action hides it; 14 seconds of renewed inactivity shows it again. Reading, hiding, cruise, failure and any secret approach cancel pending timers. Returning starts a fresh idle window. The help button stops cruise and can show instructions immediately without resetting distance. Exploration guidance invites reading a star/title, not pressing beyond the Earth boundary. There is one cancellable timeout, no polling/storage, no focus capture or live announcement, and no endlessly repeating animation. Reduced motion/pause retain static glyphs.

The 59 article IDs, saved coordinates, visit seeds, bodies, capacity-based registry, canonical pages, monthly Velog refresh and deployment workflows are unchanged. The entire archive remains available without WebGL. Not every title is shown simultaneously: limited card space and actual visibility still apply, and sideways looking reveals additional stars.

## Verification

- Automated regressions exercise camera-depth visibility, real Earth occlusion, current-position hits after camera movement, long-session/clock-reset scheduling, adaptive rectangle packing, updated retained labels, large native slider CSS/semantics, initial/repeated idle timing, cancellation, rapid input, disposal and explicit help after cruise.
- Ordinary-route projection checks cover 18 positions between 15% and 100%, five visit seeds and desktop/mobile layouts. Every sampled position has at least one readable title; each sampled mobile journey exposes at least five distinct real articles without sideways input. These are geometry regressions, not a claim that all articles fit in one forward view.
- Browser checks at 1280×720 and 390×844: actual article titles at 20%, near 83% and at 100%; connectors remain attached; a drag 18px above the visible track moved the native slider from 20% to 83.1%; initial idle guidance appeared, disappeared on input and returned with reading-specific copy after inactivity. `기울어가는 것` opened from the Earth-end title into its real full article, then closed back to the same route. Later wheel input still showed writing without a page refresh. Browser warning/error logs were empty.
- The clock-restart case is covered by code inspection and deterministic regressions. The automated in-app browser kept `document.visibilityState` visible when another background tab was opened, so this is not a claim of physical-device sleep/resume testing. The enlarged slider was dragged using the browser's pointer automation, not a physical touchscreen.
- Full test/build counts and production artifact verification are recorded in the delivery response. SEO build retains 59 complete articles, 17 photos and 63 indexable pages; no dependency, texture or GPU draw batch is added.
